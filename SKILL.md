# Catalysts Recruitment Screening

## Purpose

Start at the point where HR receives a JD/TOR and candidate CV(s).

Core workflow:

**JD/TOR + CV(s) -> CV Screening -> StandardSDS if available -> HR Recommendation**

This is decision support for HR. It does not replace recruiter, hiring-manager, technical-interviewer, or final-panel judgement.

## Inputs

Required:
- JD/TOR
- One or more candidate CVs

Optional:
- StandardSDS report
- Recruiter notes
- Hiring-manager clarification
- Role-specific screening criteria

If JD/TOR or CVs are missing, ask for them.

If StandardSDS is missing, continue without it.

---

## SDS terminology — keep these separate

There are two different meanings of SDS in the recruitment process.

### 1. SDS Profile Definition

**Skills + Domain + Seniority**

This describes the requirements of the recruitment role.

### 2. StandardSDS

**Self-Directed Search**, the Holland/RIASEC interest assessment.

StandardSDS is supplementary candidate information.

Never use StandardSDS alone to:
- shortlist a candidate
- reject a candidate
- claim technical competence
- claim job performance
- claim personality
- claim leadership capability
- claim motivation
- claim culture fit

Use StandardSDS only to identify role-relevant areas to explore and validate.

Never confuse StandardSDS with SDS Profile Definition.

---

# 1. Understand the JD/TOR

Extract only what the JD/TOR actually states.

Identify:

- Role title
- Role purpose
- Key responsibilities
- Essential experience
- Sector/domain experience
- Technical/functional skills
- Education/qualification requirements
- Seniority/level
- People-management requirements
- Stakeholder-management requirements
- Location/travel requirements
- Language requirements
- Behavioural competencies explicitly stated
- Other explicit requirements

Separate:

**Must-have / Essential**

from:

**Good-to-have / Preferred**

Do not invent requirements that are not present in the JD/TOR.

If the JD/TOR is ambiguous, state the ambiguity rather than silently assuming a requirement.

---

# 2. Screen each CV independently

For each candidate, identify evidence relating to:

- Current/most recent role
- Relevant previous roles
- Total relevant experience
- Functional experience
- Sector/domain experience
- Responsibilities performed
- Projects or assignments
- Technical/functional skills
- Education/qualifications
- Team-management experience
- Stakeholder exposure
- Geography/location
- Career progression
- Relevant achievements
- Other evidence directly connected to the JD/TOR

Do not treat a job title as proof of a skill.

Look for evidence in:
- responsibilities
- achievements
- projects
- tools
- scope
- scale
- outcomes

---

# 3. Requirement-by-requirement comparison

Use these evidence labels:

- **Evident** — clearly supported by the CV
- **Partially Evident** — some relevant evidence exists but it is incomplete
- **Not Evident** — the CV does not provide evidence
- **Not Applicable** — requirement does not apply

Use these assessment labels:

- **Strong Match**
- **Match**
- **Partial Match**
- **Gap**
- **Not Evident**

Important:

**Not mentioned in a CV is not automatically proof that the candidate does not have the experience.**

Where evidence is missing, use:

**Not evidenced in CV**

or:

**Requires validation**

Do not manufacture facts.

---

# 4. Candidate assessment

For every candidate assess:

## A. Overall alignment

How closely the available evidence aligns with the role.

## B. Relevant strengths

Identify the strongest evidence supporting the candidate.

Focus on:
- relevant domain experience
- relevant responsibilities
- relevant scale
- required technical/functional capability
- comparable seniority
- stakeholder exposure
- demonstrated outcomes

## C. Gaps

Separate:

### Confirmed gap
The CV clearly indicates that the requirement is not met.

### Potential gap
Available information suggests a possible gap but does not establish it.

### Validation required
The CV does not provide enough information to determine.

## D. Validation areas

List questions or areas HR/hiring managers should validate in the next stage.

Examples:
- depth of experience
- actual ownership versus support role
- team size managed
- scale/budget/client exposure
- domain depth
- technical capability
- location/travel feasibility
- availability/notice period
- compensation, where relevant and supplied as a screening criterion

Do not create unnecessary questions.

Focus on material uncertainties that could change the recommendation.

---

# 5. StandardSDS review

Only perform this section when a StandardSDS report is provided.

Extract, where available:

- Summary Code
- RIASEC dimensions/scores
- Dominant interest areas
- Relevant patterns described in the report

Then identify:

### What may be useful to explore for this role?

Connect the SDS information to actual work demands in the JD/TOR.

For example, if the role requires substantial:
- social interaction
- stakeholder engagement
- structured administration
- entrepreneurial activity
- analytical work

identify relevant interest areas as **topics for exploration**, not proof of ability.

Then provide **2–4 role-relevant validation questions**.

Use wording such as:

> "The StandardSDS indicates stronger interest in X. This may be useful to explore because the role involves Y. Validate through interview evidence rather than treating the assessment as a selection criterion."

Never write:

> "SDS proves the candidate is suitable."

Never write:

> "The candidate should be rejected because of the SDS."

Avoid unstructured "culture fit" conclusions.

If the organisation has explicitly defined values/behaviours, use those stated criteria and seek behavioural evidence.

---

# 6. HR recommendation

Use one of the following recommendations.

## STRONG SHORTLIST

Use when:
- core must-have requirements are clearly evidenced
- there are no material unexplained gaps
- experience/domain/seniority are broadly aligned
- available evidence supports moving the candidate forward

## SHORTLIST

Use when:
- most core requirements are evidenced
- gaps are minor or reasonably verifiable
- the candidate is sufficiently aligned to proceed

## HOLD / FURTHER INFORMATION

Use when:
- the candidate appears potentially relevant
- one or more important requirements cannot yet be established
- additional information could materially change the decision

## DO NOT SHORTLIST

Use when:
- one or more material must-have requirements are clearly not met
- there is a clear material mismatch with the role
- available evidence does not justify progressing the candidate

## INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION

Use when:
- the CV is too incomplete
- the JD/TOR is too incomplete
- essential evidence is unavailable
- a responsible recommendation cannot be made

Do not use a numeric percentage score as the primary hiring decision.

The central question is:

> **Does the available evidence justify moving this candidate to the next stage?**

# 8. Multiple-candidate screening

When multiple CVs are provided:

1. Assess every candidate independently first.
2. Do not allow one candidate's profile to change another candidate's evidence assessment.
3. After individual assessments, provide a comparative summary.

Use:

| Candidate | Overall Assessment | Key Strength | Key Gap | StandardSDS | HR Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Candidate A | Strong Match | ... | ... | Available / Not Available | Strong Shortlist |
| Candidate B | Match | ... | ... | Available / Not Available | Shortlist |
| Candidate C | Partial Match | ... | ... | Available / Not Available | Hold |

If useful, identify:
- strongest evidence against must-have criteria
- candidates requiring additional information
- candidates with clear material gaps

Do not create a false ranking where the evidence does not support one.

---

# 9. Evidence discipline

The following rules are mandatory.

## Never fabricate

Do not invent:
- employers
- dates
- years of experience
- skills
- qualifications
- project outcomes
- team size
- reporting relationships
- salary
- location
- notice period
- achievements
- assessment results

## Distinguish evidence from inference

Use:
- **JD requires...**
- **CV states...**
- **Not evidenced in CV...**
- **Requires validation...**
- **Based on the supplied StandardSDS...**

Avoid presenting assumptions as facts.

## Job relevance

Only use information that is relevant to the role.

Do not use or infer protected or sensitive personal characteristics as selection criteria, including:
- caste
- religion
- race/ethnicity
- gender/sex
- sexual orientation
- marital/family status
- health/medical information
- disability unless directly relevant to an explicitly stated lawful job requirement/accommodation process
- political affiliation
- age unless an explicitly lawful job requirement has been supplied

Do not infer these characteristics from:
- names
- photographs
- locations
- education
- language
- other indirect signals

---

# 10. Recruitment SLA reference

For the established recruitment process:

**HR Screening target SLA: 3 working days**

Keep HR screening separate from later stages such as:
- Hiring Manager Screening
- Technical Interview
- Technical Assessment
- Final Panel
- Final Decision
- HR Checks
- Offer Release
- Candidate Acceptance
- Joining

Do not invent SLA values for other stages unless they are supplied in the task/reference.

---

# 11. Quality standard

A high-quality screening report should be:

- evidence-based
- concise enough for HR operations
- detailed enough for auditability
- consistent across candidates
- transparent about missing information
- focused on must-have requirements
- free from unsupported assumptions
- useful for the next recruitment decision

Do not overvalue keywords.

A CV that repeats JD terminology without evidence should not automatically receive a strong assessment.

Do not over-penalise a candidate because a CV is brief.

If an important requirement is unclear, mark it for validation where appropriate.

Do not let StandardSDS override clear JD/CV evidence.

---

# 12. Final decision principle

The Skill is a decision-support tool for HR.

It should answer:

> **What does the evidence tell us?**

> **What is missing?**

> **What should HR validate?**

> **Is there enough evidence to move this candidate to the next stage?**

The final hiring decision remains with the organisation's authorised hiring process.

---

# Suggested HR prompts

### Single candidate

"Screen this CV against the attached JD/TOR and give me the HR recommendation."

### Multiple candidates

"Screen all these CVs against the JD/TOR. Assess each candidate independently and then give me a comparative summary."

### With StandardSDS

"Screen the CV against the JD/TOR and use the attached StandardSDS only as supplementary information. Give me the HR recommendation and areas to validate."

### Short output

"Give me only the key strengths, key gaps, validation areas and HR recommendation."

### Evidence-based screening

"Do not rank candidates only by keywords. Tell me whether the available evidence justifies moving each candidate to the next stage."
